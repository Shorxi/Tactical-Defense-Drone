Scientific Composition, Gelation & Rheology

Scientific caution: The material-specific viscosity and environmental behavior cannot be determined from the chemical names alone. They depend on formulation, molecular weight, temperature, shear rate, calcium concentration, mixing, curing time, and test method. The calculator below is an illustrative rheology model, not a validated safety or performance value.

1. Biological and Chemical Composition

The dual-fluid concept uses sodium alginate and calcium chloride.

  • Tank A (Base): Sodium alginate (NaC6H7O6).
    Alginate is a water-soluble polysaccharide associated with brown algae. Its solution viscosity depends strongly on concentration, molecular weight, temperature, and shear rate.
  • Tank B (Crosslinker): Calcium chloride (CaCl2).
    A highly soluble inorganic salt that supplies divalent calcium ions.

2. Ion-Exchange Gelation

When the two liquids contact one another, Ca2+ ions can crosslink alginate chains into a calcium-alginate network often described by the “egg-box” model. The rate and final mechanics are formulation-dependent and should be established experimentally.

3. Simplified Reaction Representation

2 NaC6H7O6 (aq) + CaCl2 (aq) → Ca(C6H7O6)2 (gel/solid network) + 2 NaCl (aq)

This is a simplified stoichiometric representation. Real alginate is a polymer with composition and molecular-weight distributions.

4. Apparent Dynamic Viscosity

For a non-Newtonian, shear-thinning material, an illustrative power-law model is:

ηapp = K · γ̇(n−1)

Here ηapp is apparent dynamic viscosity in Pa·s, K is the consistency index in Pa·sn, γ̇ is shear rate in s−1, and n is the flow-behavior index. A rheometer is required to identify K and n for the actual formulation.

5. Environmental and Safety Qualification

  • Alginate-based materials may be biodegradable, but degradation time is condition-dependent.
  • “Non-toxic,” “harmless,” and “100% eco-friendly” are not automatic conclusions and require formulation-specific toxicology, exposure, persistence, and ecological testing.
  • Salt concentration, pH, impurities, additives, and the affected environment must be assessed.
